Near a critical point, a nonlinear system looks like its linear approximation. When that approximation is robust, its phase portrait (@sec-math-differential-equations-linear-systems-linear-phase-portraits) predicts the nonlinear picture near the point.

::: {#def-linearization-system .definition name="Linearization at a critical point"}
Let $(a,b)$ be a critical point of $x'=f(x,y)$, $y'=g(x,y)$. The *Jacobian* there is
$$
J=\begin{pmatrix}f_x&f_y\\g_x&g_y\end{pmatrix}\Bigg|_{(a,b)},
$$
and the *linearized system* is $\mathbf u'=J\mathbf u$ for $\mathbf u=(x-a,y-b)$. It comes from dropping the higher-order terms of the Taylor expansions of $f$ and $g$ at $(a,b)$, where $f=g=0$.

```{=latex}
\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Sketching a nonlinear $2\times2$ system by linearization}
\begin{algorithmic}
\INPUT $x'=f(x,y)$, $y'=g(x,y)$
\STATE find all critical points: solve $f=g=0$
\FOR{each critical point $(a,b)$}
  \STATE $J\gets$ the Jacobian at $(a,b)$; compute $T=\operatorname{tr}J$, $\Delta=\det J$
  \STATE classify $J$ in the trace--determinant plane
  \IF{the type is structurally stable}
    \STATE draw the linear portrait near $(a,b)$ (eigenlines for nodes and saddles)
  \ELSE
    \STATE mark the type as undecided by linearization
  \ENDIF
\ENDFOR
\STATE join the local pictures, using nullclines and the direction of the field
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
```

:::

::: {#def-structural-stability .definition name="Structurally stable and borderline types"}
A type of critical point of a linear system is *structurally stable* if small changes of the matrix do not change it: saddles, nodes with distinct eigenvalues and spirals are structurally stable. The *borderline* types, which lie on the curves $T=0$ or $T^2=4\Delta$ of the trace–determinant plane, are not: centers, stars and defective nodes. (Points with $\Delta=0$ are degenerate.)
:::

:::

::: {#thm-linearization .theorem name="Linearization theorem"}
If the linearization at a critical point is structurally stable (a saddle, a node with distinct eigenvalues, or a spiral), then near that point the nonlinear system has a critical point of the same type and the same stability. If it is borderline, the nonlinear type may differ: a linear center can become a spiral of either stability or stay a center, and a star or defective node can become a node or a spiral of the same stability.
:::

::: {#exm-competing-species .example name="Competing species"}
Two species compete for food:
$$
x'=x(3-x-2y),\qquad y'=y(2-x-y),\qquad x,y\ge0 .
$$
The critical points and Jacobians are:

1. $(0,0)$: $J=\begin{pmatrix}3&0\\0&2\end{pmatrix}$, an unstable node.
2. $(3,0)$: $J=\begin{pmatrix}-3&-6\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}$, eigenvalues $-3,-1$: a stable node.
3. $(0,2)$: $J=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\-2&-2\end{pmatrix}$, eigenvalues $-1,-2$: a stable node.
4. $(1,1)$: $J=\begin{pmatrix}-1&-2\\-1&-1\end{pmatrix}$, $\Delta=-1$: a saddle.

All four are structurally stable, so by @thm-linearization the nonlinear picture near each point is the linear one. The saddle's two incoming trajectories divide the quadrant: depending on the starting point, one species survives and the other dies out (*competitive exclusion*).
:::
