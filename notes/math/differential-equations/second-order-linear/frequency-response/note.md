A damped system driven by $\cos\omega t$ settles into a sinusoid whose amplitude depends on $\omega$. Plotting that amplitude against $\omega$ shows at once which frequencies the system amplifies.

::: {#def-frequency-response .definition name="Frequency response"}
For the stable system $mx''+bx'+kx=F_0\cos\omega t$, the periodic solution is $x_p=F_0\,g(\omega)\cos(\omega t-\phi(\omega))$ (@thm-sinusoidal-response) with
$$
g(\omega)=\frac{1}{|p(i\omega)|}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{(k-m\omega^2)^2+b^2\omega^2}},\qquad \phi(\omega)=\arg\bigl(k-m\omega^2+ib\omega\bigr).
$$
The graph of $g$ is the *amplitude response*, the graph of $\phi$ the *phase response*.
:::

::: {#thm-practical-resonance .theorem name="Practical resonance"}
If $b^2<2mk$, the amplitude response $g$ has a unique maximum at
$$
\omega_r=\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}-\frac{b^2}{2m^2}}>0 .
$$
If $b^2\ge2mk$, $g$ is decreasing on $[0,\infty)$ and there is no practical resonance.
:::

::: {.proof}
1. $g$ is largest where $h(u)=(k-mu)^2+b^2u$ is smallest, with $u=\omega^2\ge0$.

2. $h'(u)=-2m(k-mu)+b^2=2m^2u-(2mk-b^2)$, which is increasing in $u$; so $h$ is convex with minimum at $u^*=(2mk-b^2)/(2m^2)$.

3. If $b^2<2mk$ then $u^*>0$ and $\omega_r=\sqrt{u^*}$ is the unique maximiser of $g$.

4. If $b^2\ge2mk$ then $h'(u)\ge0$ for $u\ge0$, so $h$ is increasing and $g$ decreasing.
:::

::: {#exm-practical-resonance .example name="A lightly damped spring"}
With $m=1$, $k=1$, $b=\tfrac12$: $\omega_r=\sqrt{1-\tfrac18}\approx0.935$ and $g(\omega_r)\approx2.07$, compared with $g(0)=1$. A force at the resonant frequency is amplified about twice as much as a constant force. The phase lag is $\pi/2$ at $\omega=\omega_0=1$ and increases towards $\pi$ for fast driving.
:::

::: {#def-rlc-circuit .definition name="RLC circuit"}
A resistor $R$, inductor $L$ and capacitor $C$ in series with a voltage source $E(t)$ carry a charge $q(t)$ on the capacitor that satisfies
$$
L\,q''+R\,q'+\frac1C\,q=E(t),
$$
by Kirchhoff's voltage law; the current is $I=q'$. It is the spring equation with $m\leftrightarrow L$, $b\leftrightarrow R$, $k\leftrightarrow1/C$.
:::

A series RLC circuit driven by a voltage $E(t)$. Its charge equation is the mass–spring–dashpot equation with $L$, $R$, $1/C$ in place of $m$, $b$, $k$.

::: {#def-complex-impedance .definition name="Complex impedance"}
For the input $E(t)=E_0\cos\omega t$, the *complex impedance* of the series circuit is
$$
Z(\omega)=R+i\left(\omega L-\frac{1}{\omega C}\right),
$$
and the periodic current is $I=\operatorname{Re}\bigl(E_0e^{i\omega t}/Z\bigr)$. Its amplitude $E_0/|Z|$ is largest at $\omega=1/\sqrt{LC}$, where $Z=R$.
:::

::: {#rmk-frequency-response-1 .remark name="Where the force enters matters"}
Driving a system through the spring (moving the spring's far end by $y=\cos\omega t$) gives $mx''+bx'+kx=ky$; driving through the dashpot gives $mx''+bx'+kx=by'$. The left side is the same, but the input differs, so the gain changes: with the dashpot input, $g$ is largest exactly at $\omega_0$ and equals $1$ there. Always identify the physical input before computing a gain.
:::
