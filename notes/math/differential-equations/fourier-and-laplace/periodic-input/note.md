A linear system responds to each harmonic of a periodic input separately. Adding the responses gives the periodic response, and any harmonic close to a natural frequency is singled out.

::: {#thm-periodic-input-response .theorem name="Response to periodic input"}
Let $p$ have real coefficients and let $f$ have period $2L$ with Fourier series $\frac{a_0}2+\sum_{n\ge1}(a_n\cos n\omega t+b_n\sin n\omega t)$. If $p(in\omega)\neq0$ for every $n\ge0$ with a nonzero coefficient, then a periodic solution of $p(D)x=f$ is
$$
x_p=\frac{a_0}{2p(0)}+\sum_{n\ge1}\operatorname{Re}\frac{(a_n-ib_n)\,e^{in\omega t}}{p(in\omega)} ,
$$
provided the series and its derivatives up to the order of $p$ converge uniformly.
:::

::: {.proof}
1. As in @prp-sinusoid-identity, $a_n\cos n\omega t+b_n\sin n\omega t=\operatorname{Re}\bigl((a_n-ib_n)e^{in\omega t}\bigr)$.

2. By @thm-exponential-response-formula and complex replacement, $\operatorname{Re}\bigl((a_n-ib_n)e^{in\omega t}/p(in\omega)\bigr)$ solves $p(D)x$ equal to that term, and $a_0/(2p(0))$ solves $p(D)x=a_0/2$.

3. By superposition (@thm-superposition-linear) every partial sum of the series solves $p(D)x$ equal to the corresponding partial sum of the input.

4. Uniform convergence of the series and its derivatives lets us apply $p(D)$ term by term in the limit, so $x_p$ solves $p(D)x=f$; each term has period $2L$, so $x_p$ does too.
:::

The periodic response to a square wave for $x''+10x$ (scaled): about three oscillations per period of the input, the near-resonant third harmonic.

:::

::: {#def-harmonic-resonance .definition name="Resonance with a harmonic"}
If $p(in\omega)=0$ for some $n$ whose coefficient in the input is nonzero, the system has *pure resonance* with the $n$-th harmonic, and that harmonic produces an unbounded response (@thm-generalized-erf). If $|p(in\omega)|$ is merely small, the $n$-th harmonic dominates the periodic response even when it is a small part of the input (*near resonance*).
:::

::: {#exm-square-wave-input .example name="A spring driven by a square wave"}
Drive $x''+10x=\operatorname{sq}(t)$, where $\operatorname{sq}$ is the square wave $\frac4\pi\sum_{n\text{ odd}}\frac{\sin nt}{n}$ of period $2\pi$. Since $p(in)=10-n^2$,
$$
x_p=\frac4\pi\sum_{n\text{ odd}}\frac{\sin nt}{n\,(10-n^2)} .
$$
The amplitudes are about $0.141$ for $n=1$, $0.424$ for $n=3$, $0.017$ for $n=5$. The natural frequency $\sqrt{10}\approx3.16$ is close to $3$, so the third harmonic dominates: the mass oscillates about three times per period of the input.
:::
