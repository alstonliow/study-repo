The response of a linear time-invariant system to a single impulse determines its response to every input: the input is a superposition of impulses, so the output is the same superposition of impulse responses.

::: {#def-unit-impulse-response .definition name="Unit step and unit impulse response"}
For the system $p(D)x=q$ with rest initial conditions, the *unit step response* is the solution for $q=u(t)$ and the *unit impulse response* (or *weight function*) $w(t)$ is the solution for $q=\delta(t)$. By time invariance the impulse response is the derivative of the step response.
:::

::: {#prp-impulse-response-second-order .proposition name="Impulse response of a second-order system"}
The unit impulse response of $mx''+bx'+kx=\delta(t)$ is $w(t)=u(t)\,\tilde w(t)$, where $\tilde w$ solves the homogeneous equation with
$$
\tilde w(0)=0,\qquad \tilde w'(0)=\frac1m .
$$
:::

::: {.proof}
1. For $t<0$ the input and the solution are $0$; for $t>0$ the input is $0$, so $x$ solves the homogeneous equation there.

2. Integrating the equation from $-\varepsilon$ to $\varepsilon$ gives $m\bigl(x'(\varepsilon)-x'(-\varepsilon)\bigr)+b\bigl(x(\varepsilon)-x(-\varepsilon)\bigr)+k\int_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}x\,dt=1$.

3. If $x$ stays bounded, $x$ is continuous at $0$ (only $x''$ contains a delta), so the last two terms tend to $0$ as $\varepsilon\to0$, leaving $m\,x'(0^+)=1$ while $x(0^+)=0$.
:::

Impulse response of $x''+\tfrac12x'+x=\delta(t)$: zero before the blow, then a homogeneous solution starting at $0$ with slope $1/m=1$.

:::

::: {#def-causal-convolution .definition name="Convolution of causal functions" related="def-convolution"}
For functions on $t\ge0$, the *convolution* is
$$
(f*g)(t)=\int_0^tf(\tau)\,g(t-\tau)\,d\tau .
$$
It is commutative, associative and distributes over addition; for example $1*1=t$ and $t*t=\tfrac{t^3}{6}$.
:::

::: {#thm-greens-formula .theorem name="Green's formula"}
If $w$ is the unit impulse response of $p(D)x=q$, then for any piecewise continuous input $f$ on $t\ge0$, the solution with rest initial conditions is
$$
x(t)=(w*f)(t)=\int_0^tw(t-\tau)\,f(\tau)\,d\tau .
$$
:::

::: {.proof}
1. Approximate $f$ on $[0,t]$ by impulses: $f(t)\approx\sum_kf(\tau_k)\,\Delta\tau\,\delta(t-\tau_k)$ with $\tau_k=k\,\Delta\tau$.

2. By time invariance the response to $\delta(t-\tau_k)$ is $w(t-\tau_k)$, and by superposition the response to the sum is $\sum_kf(\tau_k)\,w(t-\tau_k)\,\Delta\tau$.

3. This is a Riemann sum for $\int_0^tw(t-\tau)f(\tau)\,d\tau$, which it approaches as $\Delta\tau\to0$. (For first order, @thm-constant-coefficient-first-order with $y_0=0$ is exactly this formula with $w=e^{-kt}$; the Laplace transform gives a rigorous proof for every order.)
:::

Green's formula: the step input is a train of small impulses; each produces a shifted copy of the impulse response (grey, three shown), and their sum is the step response $w*1$.

:::

::: {#exm-greens-formula .example name="Two convolutions"}
1. For $x'+x=f$, $w=e^{-t}$. With the step input $f=1$: $x=\int_0^te^{-(t-\tau)}\,d\tau=1-e^{-t}$.
2. For $x''+x=f$, $w=\sin t$ (since $\tilde w(0)=0$, $\tilde w'(0)=1$). With $f=1$: $x=\int_0^t\sin(t-\tau)\,d\tau=1-\cos t$, which indeed solves $x''+x=1$ with $x(0)=x'(0)=0$.
:::
