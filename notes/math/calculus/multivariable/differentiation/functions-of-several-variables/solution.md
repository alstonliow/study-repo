(Sketches described in words.)

a) Level curves $x+y=1-c$: parallel lines of slope $-1$. The graph is the plane $x+y+z=1$; in the first octant it is the triangle with vertices $(1,0,0),(0,1,0),(0,0,1)$, whose sides are the traces in the coordinate planes.

b) Level curves $x^2+y^2=c^2$: circles of radius $c$, equally spaced. The graph is the cone $z=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$; its traces in the $xz$- and $yz$-planes are the lines $z=x$, $z=y$, and in the $xy$-plane the origin.

c) Level curves: circles of radius $\sqrt c$, crowding together as $c$ grows. The graph is the paraboloid $z=x^2+y^2$; its traces in the $xz$- and $yz$-planes are the parabolas $z=x^2$, $z=y^2$.

d) Level curves: circles $x^2+y^2=1-c$ ($c\le1$). The graph is the paraboloid with top $(0,0,1)$, opening downwards; in the first octant it is the piece over the quarter disk, with traces $z=1-x^2$, $z=1-y^2$ and the quarter circle $x^2+y^2=1$ in the $xy$-plane.

e) Level curves: hyperbolas $x^2-y^2=c$ (opening left–right for $c>0$, up–down for $c<0$) and the lines $y=\pm x$ for $c=0$. The graph is a saddle.
