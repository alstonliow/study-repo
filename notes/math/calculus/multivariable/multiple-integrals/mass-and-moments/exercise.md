::: {.problem #prob-mit1802f07-7-q3 origin="external" source="mit1802f07-7-q3"}
In general, the moment of inertia around an axis (a line) $L$ is
$$I_L=\iint_R\operatorname{dist}(\cdot,L)^2\,\delta\,dA .$$
The collection of lines parallel to the $y$-axis have the form $x=a$. Let $I=I_y$ be the usual moment of inertia around the $y$-axis
$$I=\iint_Rx^2\,\delta\,dA .$$
Let $\bar I$ be the moment of inertia around the axis $x=\bar x$, where $(\bar x,\bar y)$ is the center of mass. Show that
$$I=\bar I+M\bar x^2 .$$
This is known as the parallel axis theorem.
:::
