If $S$ is an $m\times m$ real-symmetric matrix with a "simple" (multiplicity $=1$) eigenvalue $\lambda$ and corresponding eigenvector $q$ ($Sq=\lambda q)$, normalized to $q^{T}q=1$, then the "Hellman--Feynman theorem" states that $d\lambda=q^{T}dS\:q$ for a change $dS$ in the matrix $S$.

(a) Derive the Hellman--Feynman theorem by considering the differentials of both sides of the equations $d(\lambda=q^{T}Sq)$ and $d(q^{T}q=1$).

(b) What is the gradient $\nabla\lambda$ with respect to $S$, for the usual Frobenius inner product $\nabla\lambda\cdot dS=\operatorname{tr}((\nabla\lambda)^{T}dS)$
