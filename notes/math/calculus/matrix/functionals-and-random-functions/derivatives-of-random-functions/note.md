A *stochastic program* returns a random output whose distribution depends on a parameter $p$: a simulation with random events, a randomly sampled loss. What one usually wants is the derivative of the *expected* output, and what one can usually afford is an *unbiased estimate* of it from a few random runs.

::: {#def-unbiased-gradient-estimator .definition name="Stochastic program and unbiased gradient estimator"}

A *stochastic program* is a family of random variables $X(p)$, $p\in\mathbb{R}$, on a probability space $(\Omega,P)$, with $\mathbb{E}[X(p)]$ finite. An *unbiased gradient estimator* is a random variable $X'(p)$ with

$$
\mathbb{E}\bigl[X'(p)\bigr]=\frac{d}{dp}\,\mathbb{E}\bigl[X(p)\bigr].
$$

Among unbiased estimators one prefers those with small variance, since the error of an average of $k$ independent samples is about $\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}X'(p)/k}$.

:::

::: {#rmk-derivatives-of-random-functions-1 .remark name="Independent samples give useless differences"}

Sampling $X(p)$ and $X(p+\delta p)$ independently and taking $(X(p+\delta p)-X(p))/\delta p$ is unbiased only up to $O(\delta p)$, and its variance is of order $1/\delta p^2$: the difference of two independent random numbers does not shrink with $\delta p$. A useful stochastic derivative must *couple* the two runs, using the same randomness for $p$ and $p+\delta p$.

:::

::: {#prp-reparameterization-gradient .proposition name="The reparameterization (pathwise) estimator" generalizes="prp-reparameterization"}

Suppose $X(p)=h(p,\omega)$, where $\omega$ has a distribution that does not depend on $p$, $h(\cdot,\omega)$ is differentiable for almost every $\omega$, and $\lvert\partial h/\partial p\rvert\le M(\omega)$ near $p$ with $\mathbb{E}M<\infty$. Then

$$
\frac{d}{dp}\,\mathbb{E}[X(p)]=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\frac{\partial h}{\partial p}(p,\omega)\Bigr],
$$

so $X'(p)=\partial h/\partial p\,(p,\omega)$ is an unbiased gradient estimator, computable by AD through the program with $\omega$ held fixed.

:::

::: {.proof}

1. $\mathbb{E}[X(p)]=\int_\Omega h(p,\omega)\,dP(\omega)$, and the measure $P$ does not depend on $p$.

2. The difference quotient $\bigl(h(p+\delta,\omega)-h(p,\omega)\bigr)/\delta$ equals $\partial_ph(\xi,\omega)$ for some $\xi$ between $p$ and $p+\delta$ by the mean value theorem, so it is bounded by $M(\omega)$.

3. By dominated convergence, the limit $\delta\to0$ may be taken inside the integral, giving $\int\partial_ph\,dP$.

:::

::: {#exm-derivatives-of-random-functions-1 .example name="An exponential random variable"}

An exponential variable with mean $p$ can be generated by inversion: $X(p)=-p\log(1-\omega)$ with $\omega$ uniform on $(0,1)$. With $\omega$ fixed, $\partial X/\partial p=-\log(1-\omega)=X/p$.

1. $\mathbb{E}[X'(p)]=\mathbb{E}[X]/p=1=\frac{d}{dp}p$.

2. For $Y=X^2$, the chain rule gives $Y'=2X\cdot X/p=2X^2/p$, with mean $2\cdot2p^2/p=4p=\frac{d}{dp}(2p^2)$, since $\mathbb{E}X^2=2p^2$.

With $p=2$ and $10^6$ samples in Python, the averages are $1.001$ and $8.03$, against the exact $1$ and $8$.

:::

:::

::: {#prp-score-function-estimator .proposition name="The score-function estimator" related="thm-policy-gradient"}

Let $X$ take values in a finite set with probabilities $P_p(x)>0$ differentiable in $p$, and let $f$ be any function. Then

$$
\frac{d}{dp}\,\mathbb{E}_p\bigl[f(X)\bigr]=\mathbb{E}_p\Bigl[f(X)\,\frac{\partial}{\partial p}\log P_p(X)\Bigr].
$$

The same holds for densities under conditions that allow differentiating under the integral.

:::

::: {.proof}

1. $\mathbb{E}_p[f(X)]=\sum_xf(x)P_p(x)$ is a finite sum, so $\frac{d}{dp}\mathbb{E}_p[f(X)]=\sum_xf(x)\,\partial_pP_p(x)$.

2. Write $\partial_pP_p(x)=P_p(x)\,\partial_p\log P_p(x)$.

3. The sum becomes $\sum_xP_p(x)\,f(x)\,\partial_p\log P_p(x)$, the expectation on the right.

:::

::: {#exm-derivatives-of-random-functions-2 .example name="A Bernoulli variable"}

Let $X\sim\mathrm{Ber}(p)$, so $\mathbb{E}X=p$ and the derivative is $1$.

1. By inversion, $X=1$ if $\omega\ge1-p$ and $0$ otherwise. For each fixed $\omega$ this is a step function of $p$, with derivative $0$ almost everywhere: the pathwise estimator of @prp-reparameterization-gradient is $0$, which is biased. The domination condition fails because $h$ jumps.

2. The score is $\partial_p\log P_p(1)=1/p$ and $\partial_p\log P_p(0)=-1/(1-p)$. The score-function estimator $X\,\partial_p\log P_p(X)$ equals $1/p$ with probability $p$ and $0$ otherwise, with mean $1$, as required. Its variance $1/p-1$ is large when $p$ is small, the usual weakness of score-function estimators.

:::
