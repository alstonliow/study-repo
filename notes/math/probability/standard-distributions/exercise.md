You need a die but don’t have one. Your friend suggests tossing five fair
coins, counting the Heads (in $\{0,\dots,5\}$) and adding one to get a number
in $\{1,\dots,6\}$. Discuss this suggestion. Can you think of any other way
in which a set of fair coins (possibly more than six) can be used to simulate
the throw of a fair die?

::: {.problem #prob-probability-2-07 origin="external" source="fit1058-cn-10.12-q7"}
Suppose $X\sim\mathrm{Bin}(16,0.5)$.

(a) Your friend claims that, because $p=\frac12$, half the trials must
    be successes and half failures. What is the probability that this
    actually happens?

(b) What is the probability that $X$ is at least two standard
    deviations away from the mean?

(c) What is the probability that $X$ is at least three standard
    deviations away from the mean?

(d) How do these exact probabilities compare with the bounds given by
    Chebyshev’s Theorem?
:::

::: {.problem #prob-probability-2-08 origin="external" source="fit1058-cn-10.12-q8"}
Meteors arrive randomly and independently. The Eta Aquariids meteor shower
has, at its peak and in idealised conditions, an average rate of $0.83$
visible meteors per minute.

(a) What is the distribution of the number of meteors seen in a given
    minute around the peak?

(b) What is the probability that no meteors are seen in a given minute?

(c) How long should an observer be prepared to wait to have
    probability $0.95$ of seeing a meteor?
:::

::: {.problem #prob-probability-2-09 origin="external" source="fit1058-cn-10.12-q9"}
Each ball bowled to a Test cricket batter dismisses them with probability
$1.25\%$, independently.

(a) What is the distribution of the number of balls the batter faces
    until they are dismissed?

(b) What is the probability of a “golden duck” (dismissed by the
    first ball)?

(c) What is the expected length of their innings?

(d) Suppose the batter has faced 200 balls without being dismissed.
    How many further balls would you expect them to face?
:::

::: {.problem #prob-probability-2-10 origin="external" source="fit1058-cn-10.12-q10"}
For each random variable, state which distribution is the best model:
uniform, binomial, Poisson, geometric. If none fits, discuss why and suggest
an appropriate distribution.

(a) the face value of the top card in a well-shuffled standard deck
    (Ace, Jack, Queen, King $=1,11,12,13$);

(b) the number of days in a particular week on which your regular
    morning train is late (i.i.d. days; late = at least a minute after
    schedule);

(c) the number of cosmic rays that hit your laptop’s CPU during your
    next class;

(d) the number of working days you wait, from tomorrow, until your
    train arrives on time (same assumptions as (b));

(e) the number of cereal packets you buy until you get one of your
    three favourite coupons (original coupon collector’s scenario);

(f) the number of cowrie shells landing aperture-up when seven are
    thrown in Pachisi (identical, independent shells);

(g) a random digit from the first 1,002 digits after the decimal
    point of $3/7$;

(h) a random digit from the first 1,000 digits after the decimal
    point of $5/11$;

(i) a random digit from the first $10^{100}$ digits of $\pi$.
:::
