Conditioning on an event $B$ with $\Pr(B)>0$ gives $E[X\mid B]=E[X\mathbf 1_B]/\Pr(B)$. To condition on a whole σ-algebra $\mathcal G$ (the information available), we keep the two properties that matter and define the result abstractly. Throughout, $(\Omega,\mathcal F,\Pr)$ is a probability space, $\mathcal G\subseteq\mathcal F$ a sub-σ-algebra, and all (in)equalities between conditional expectations hold almost surely.

::: {#def-conditional-expectation-sigma-algebra .definition name="Conditional expectation given a σ-algebra"}

Let $X\in L^1$. A *conditional expectation* of $X$ given $\mathcal G$ is a random variable $Y=E[X\mid\mathcal G]$ with

1. $Y$ is $\mathcal G$-measurable, and $Y\in L^1$;
2. $E[Y\mathbf 1_A]=E[X\mathbf 1_A]$ for every $A\in\mathcal G$.

We write $\Pr(A\mid\mathcal G)=E[\mathbf 1_A\mid\mathcal G]$ and $E[X\mid Z]=E[X\mid\sigma(Z)]$.

:::

For $\mathcal G$ generated by a partition $G_1,G_2,\dots$ (vertical strips), $E[X\mid\mathcal G]$ (thick, flat pieces) replaces the wiggly $X$ by its average on each strip.

:::

::: {#exm-conditional-expectation-partition .example name="A countable partition"}

If $\mathcal G=\sigma(G_1,G_2,\dots)$ for disjoint events $G_n$ of positive probability covering $\Omega$, then

$$
E[X\mid\mathcal G]=\sum_nE[X\mid G_n]\,\mathbf 1_{G_n}.
$$

Indeed the right side is $\mathcal G$-measurable, integrable (by monotone convergence $E\sum_n|E[X\mid G_n]|\mathbf 1_{G_n}\le E|X|$), and every $A\in\mathcal G$ is a union of $G_n$'s, on each of which both sides have the same integral. This is the elementary definition of @sec-math-probability-random-variables-conditional-expectation.

:::

::: {#thm-conditional-expectation-exists .theorem name="Existence and uniqueness"}

For every $X\in L^1$ a conditional expectation $E[X\mid\mathcal G]$ exists, and any two versions agree almost surely.

:::

::: {.proof}

1. *Uniqueness.* If $Y,Y'$ both satisfy the definition, put $A=\{Y>Y'\}\in\mathcal G$. Then $E[(Y-Y')\mathbf 1_A]=0$ with $(Y-Y')\mathbf 1_A\ge0$, so $\Pr(A)=0$. Symmetrically $\Pr(Y<Y')=0$.

2. *The case $X\in L^2$.* $L^2(\mathcal G)$ is a closed subspace of the Hilbert space $L^2(\mathcal F)$. Let $Y$ be the orthogonal projection of $X$ onto it (@thm-l2-orthogonal-projection). Then $Y$ is $\mathcal G$-measurable, and $E[(X-Y)Z]=0$ for all $Z\in L^2(\mathcal G)$; taking $Z=\mathbf 1_A$ gives property 2.

3. *Monotonicity.* If $(X,Y)$ and $(X',Y')$ both satisfy the definition and $X'\ge X$ a.s., then $Y'\ge Y$ a.s.: for $A=\{Y'<Y\}\in\mathcal G$ we get $E[Y'\mathbf 1_A]=E[X'\mathbf 1_A]\ge E[X\mathbf 1_A]=E[Y\mathbf 1_A]$, so $E[(Y-Y')\mathbf 1_A]\le0$ with a positive integrand, hence $\Pr(A)=0$.

4. *The case $X\ge0$.* Let $Y_n$ be conditional expectations of $X\wedge n\in L^2$; by step 3 we may choose them increasing and non-negative. Put $Y_\infty=\lim Y_n$, which is $\mathcal G$-measurable. By monotone convergence, for $A\in\mathcal G$, $E[X\mathbf 1_A]=\lim E[(X\wedge n)\mathbf 1_A]=\lim E[Y_n\mathbf 1_A]=E[Y_\infty\mathbf 1_A]$. Taking $A=\Omega$ gives $E[Y_\infty]=E[X]<\infty$, so $Y_\infty<\infty$ a.s. and $Y=Y_\infty\mathbf 1_{\{Y_\infty<\infty\}}$ works.

5. *General $X\in L^1$.* Write $X=X^+-X^-$ and take $Y=E[X^+\mid\mathcal G]-E[X^-\mid\mathcal G]$.

:::

::: {#lem-conditional-expectation-function .lemma name="Conditioning on a random variable"}

If $Y$ is $\sigma(Z)$-measurable, then $Y=h(Z)$ for a Borel function $h$. In particular $E[X\mid Z]=h(Z)$ almost surely for some Borel $h$, and we write $E[X\mid Z=z]=h(z)$.

:::

This defines a conditional expectation also when $\Pr(Z=z)=0$. In practice one *guesses* the answer and checks the two defining properties.

::: {#exm-conditional-expectation-density .example name="Joint density"}

Let $(U,V)$ have joint density $f_{U,V}$, with marginal $f_U$ and conditional density $f_{V\mid U}(v\mid u)=f_{U,V}(u,v)/f_U(u)$. For Borel $h$ with $h(V)\in L^1$,

$$
E[h(V)\mid U]=g(U),\qquad g(u)=\int h(v)\,f_{V\mid U}(v\mid u)\,dv.
$$

$g(U)$ is $\sigma(U)$-measurable, and every $A\in\sigma(U)$ has the form $\{U\in B\}$, so $E[h(V)\mathbf 1_A]=\iint h(v)\mathbf 1_B(u)f_{V\mid U}(v\mid u)f_U(u)\,dv\,du=E[g(U)\mathbf 1_A]$.

:::

::: {#exm-conditional-expectation-gaussian .example name="Gaussian pair"}

Let $(X,W)$ be jointly Gaussian. Any $\sigma(W)$-measurable $Y$ with $E[Y]=E[X]$ and $X-Y$ independent of $W$ is $E[X\mid W]$, since then $E[(X-Y)\mathbf 1_A]=E[X-Y]\Pr(A)=0$ for $A\in\sigma(W)$. Try $Y=aW+b$. Uncorrelated jointly Gaussian variables are independent, so we need

$$
\operatorname{cov}(X-aW-b,\,W)=0\iff a=\frac{\operatorname{cov}(X,W)}{\operatorname{var}(W)},\qquad b=E[X]-aE[W].
$$

Hence $E[X\mid W]=E[X]+\dfrac{\operatorname{cov}(X,W)}{\operatorname{var}W}\,(W-E[W])$.

:::

::: {#prp-conditional-expectation-properties .proposition name="Properties of conditional expectation"}

For $X,X_n\in L^1$, $\mathcal H\subseteq\mathcal G\subseteq\mathcal F$:

1. $E[X\mid\mathcal G]=X$ iff $X$ is $\mathcal G$-measurable; $E[E[X\mid\mathcal G]]=E[X]$.
2. Positivity and linearity: $X\ge0\Rightarrow E[X\mid\mathcal G]\ge0$; $E[\alpha X_1+\beta X_2\mid\mathcal G]=\alpha E[X_1\mid\mathcal G]+\beta E[X_2\mid\mathcal G]$.
3. If $X$ is independent of $\mathcal G$, then $E[X\mid\mathcal G]=E[X]$.
4. Monotone convergence: $0\le X_n\uparrow X$ implies $E[X_n\mid\mathcal G]\uparrow E[X\mid\mathcal G]$. Fatou: $X_n\ge0$ implies $E[\liminf X_n\mid\mathcal G]\le\liminf E[X_n\mid\mathcal G]$. Dominated convergence: $X_n\to X$ and $|X_n|\le W\in L^1$ imply $E[X_n\mid\mathcal G]\to E[X\mid\mathcal G]$.
5. Jensen: for convex $c$ with $c(X)\in L^1$, $E[c(X)\mid\mathcal G]\ge c(E[X\mid\mathcal G])$.
6. Tower property: $E[E[X\mid\mathcal G]\mid\mathcal H]=E[X\mid\mathcal H]$.
7. $\|E[X\mid\mathcal G]\|_p\le\|X\|_p$ for $p\ge1$.
8. Taking out what is known: if $Z$ is bounded and $\mathcal G$-measurable, $E[ZX\mid\mathcal G]=Z\,E[X\mid\mathcal G]$.
9. If $\sigma(X,\mathcal G)$ is independent of $\mathcal H$, then $E[X\mid\sigma(\mathcal G,\mathcal H)]=E[X\mid\mathcal G]$.

:::

::: {.proof}

1. Each right-hand side is $\mathcal G$-measurable (resp. $\mathcal H$-measurable), integrable, and has the right integrals over sets of the σ-algebra; uniqueness (@thm-conditional-expectation-exists) identifies it. This disposes of the first line of 1, of linearity, of 3 (constants are measurable and $E[X\mathbf 1_A]=E[X]\Pr(A)$) and of 6 (the left side satisfies the defining property of $E[X\mid\mathcal H]$ because $\mathcal H\subseteq\mathcal G$). Taking $A=\Omega$ gives $E[E[X\mid\mathcal G]]=E[X]$.

2. Positivity and monotone convergence were shown in steps 3–4 of the existence proof. Fatou and dominated convergence follow exactly as for ordinary expectations, using monotone convergence and positivity.

3. Jensen follows as in the unconditional case (@thm-jensen-integrable), using positivity and linearity. For 7, apply Jensen with $c(x)=|x|^p$: $E[|E[X\mid\mathcal G]|^p]\le E[E[|X|^p\mid\mathcal G]]=E|X|^p$.

4. For 8, take $Z=\mathbf 1_B$ with $B\in\mathcal G$; for $A\in\mathcal G$, $E[Z\,E[X\mid\mathcal G]\mathbf 1_A]=E[X\mathbf 1_{A\cap B}]=E[ZX\mathbf 1_A]$. Linearity covers simple $Z$, and bounded convergence covers bounded $Z$.

5. For 9, the sets $A\cap B$ ($A\in\mathcal G$, $B\in\mathcal H$) form a π-system generating $\sigma(\mathcal G,\mathcal H)$. For such a set, using independence twice, $E[X\mathbf 1_{A\cap B}]=E[X\mathbf 1_A]\Pr(B)=E[E[X\mid\mathcal G]\mathbf 1_A]\Pr(B)=E[E[X\mid\mathcal G]\mathbf 1_{A\cap B}]$. Both sides of the identity are finite signed measures in $A\cap B$-sets that agree on a π-system, hence agree on the generated σ-algebra (@prp-integral-pi-system).

:::

::: {#lem-conditional-expectations-ui .lemma name="Conditional expectations are uniformly integrable"}

For $X\in L^1$, the family $\{E[X\mid\mathcal G]:\ \mathcal G\subseteq\mathcal F\text{ a σ-algebra}\}$ is uniformly integrable.

:::

::: {.proof}

1. Fix $\varepsilon>0$. Since $X\in L^1$ there is $\delta>0$ with $E[|X|\mathbf 1_A]<\varepsilon$ whenever $\Pr(A)<\delta$.

2. Let $Y=E[X\mid\mathcal G]$. By Jensen, $|Y|\le E[|X|\mid\mathcal G]$, so $E|Y|\le E|X|$, and by Markov $\Pr(|Y|\ge\lambda)\le E|X|/\lambda<\delta$ for $\lambda>E|X|/\delta$.

3. Since $\{|Y|\ge\lambda\}\in\mathcal G$, $E[|Y|\mathbf 1_{\{|Y|\ge\lambda\}}]\le E[E[|X|\mid\mathcal G]\mathbf 1_{\{|Y|\ge\lambda\}}]=E[|X|\mathbf 1_{\{|Y|\ge\lambda\}}]<\varepsilon$, uniformly in $\mathcal G$.

:::
