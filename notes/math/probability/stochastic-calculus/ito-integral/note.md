The integral against a martingale $M$ is defined by a Riemann sum on simple integrands and then extended by continuity in a Hilbert space, the way the Lebesgue integral is extended from simple functions.

::: {#def-simple-stochastic-integral .definition name="Integral of a simple process"}

For $M\in\mathcal M^2$ and $H=\sum_{i=1}^nH_{i-1}\mathbf 1_{(t_{i-1},t_i]}\in\mathcal E$ (@def-previsible),
$$(H\cdot M)_t=\int_0^tH\,dM=\sum_{i=1}^nH_{i-1}\big(M_{t_i\wedge t}-M_{t_{i-1}\wedge t}\big).$$

:::

::: {#prp-simple-integral-isometry .proposition name="Isometry on simple processes"}

If $M\in\mathcal M^2_c$ and $H\in\mathcal E$, then $H\cdot M\in\mathcal M^2_c$ and
$$\lVert H\cdot M\rVert^2_{\mathcal M^2}=E\Big[\int_0^\infty H_s^2\,d\langle M\rangle_s\Big].$$

:::

::: {.proof}

1. $H\cdot M$ is continuous and adapted. Each summand $H_{i-1}(M_{t_i\wedge t}-M_{t_{i-1}\wedge t})$ is a martingale because $H_{i-1}$ is bounded and $\mathcal F_{t_{i-1}}$-measurable.

2. The summands are orthogonal in $L^2$, so $E[(H\cdot M)_\infty^2]=\sum_iE\big[H_{i-1}^2(M_{t_i}-M_{t_{i-1}})^2\big]$.

3. Conditioning on $\mathcal F_{t_{i-1}}$ and using that $M^2-\langle M\rangle$ is a martingale, $E[(M_{t_i}-M_{t_{i-1}})^2\mid\mathcal F_{t_{i-1}}]=E[\langle M\rangle_{t_i}-\langle M\rangle_{t_{i-1}}\mid\mathcal F_{t_{i-1}}]$.

4. Summing gives $E\big[\int H^2\,d\langle M\rangle\big]$.

:::

::: {#def-l2-m .definition name="The space L²(M)"}

For $M\in\mathcal M^2_c$, $L^2(M)$ is the space of (classes of) previsible $H$ with
$$\lVert H\rVert_{L^2(M)}^2=E\Big[\int_0^\infty H_s^2\,d\langle M\rangle_s\Big]<\infty,\qquad(H,K)_{L^2(M)}=E\Big[\int_0^\infty H_sK_s\,d\langle M\rangle_s\Big].$$
Equivalently $L^2(M)=L^2(\Omega\times[0,\infty),\mathcal P,\Pr\otimes d\langle M\rangle)$, a Hilbert space.

:::

::: {#prp-simple-dense .proposition name="Simple processes are dense"}

For $M\in\mathcal M^2_c$ the space $\mathcal E$ is dense in $L^2(M)$.

:::

This is the usual density of simple functions in $L^2$ of a measure, applied to the measure $\Pr\otimes d\langle M\rangle$ on $(\Omega\times[0,\infty),\mathcal P)$, since $\mathcal P$ is generated by the π-system $\Pi$ of @def-previsible.

::: {#thm-ito-isometry .theorem name="Itô isometry and the stochastic integral"}

Let $M\in\mathcal M^2_c$.

1. The map $H\mapsto H\cdot M$ on $\mathcal E$ extends uniquely to an isometry $L^2(M)\to\mathcal M^2_c$.

2. For $H\in L^2(M)$, $H\cdot M$ is the unique element of $\mathcal M^2_c$ with $\langle H\cdot M,N\rangle=H\cdot\langle M,N\rangle$ for all $N\in\mathcal M^2_c$ (the right side a Lebesgue–Stieltjes integral).

3. For a stopping time $T$, $(\mathbf 1_{[0,T]}H)\cdot M=(H\cdot M)^T=H\cdot M^T$.

:::

::: {.proof}

1. By @prp-simple-integral-isometry the map is isometric on the dense subspace $\mathcal E$, and $\mathcal M^2_c$ is complete (@thm-m2-hilbert), so the isometry extends uniquely.

2. For simple $H$ the identity $\langle H\cdot M,N\rangle=H\cdot\langle M,N\rangle$ is a direct computation; both sides are continuous in $H$ (the right side by Kunita–Watanabe, @thm-kunita-watanabe), so it holds on $L^2(M)$. Uniqueness: two martingales with the same bracket against every $N$ have $\langle X-Y\rangle=0$, so $X=Y$.

3. Again check on simple processes and pass to the limit.

:::

::: {#def-ito-integral .definition name="Stochastic (Itô) integral"}

$H\cdot M$ is the *stochastic integral* of $H$ with respect to $M$, written $(H\cdot M)_t=\int_0^tH_s\,dM_s$.

:::

The integral of a martingale is a martingale; this is what makes Itô's formula (@sec-math-probability-stochastic-calculus-itos-formula) a systematic source of martingales.

::: {#def-l2-bc .definition name="Locally square-integrable integrands"}

For a continuous local martingale $M$ let $L^2_{bc}(M)$ be the previsible $H$ with $\int_0^tH_s^2\,d\langle M\rangle_s<\infty$ almost surely for all $t$.

:::

::: {#thm-ito-local-martingale .theorem name="The integral against a continuous local martingale"}

Let $M$ be a continuous local martingale.

1. For $H\in L^2_{bc}(M)$ there is a unique continuous local martingale $H\cdot M$ with $(H\cdot M)_0=0$ and $\langle H\cdot M,N\rangle=H\cdot\langle M,N\rangle$ for all continuous local martingales $N$.

2. For a stopping time $T$: $(\mathbf 1_{[0,T]}H)\cdot M=(H\cdot M)^T=H\cdot M^T$.

3. If $H\in L^2_{bc}(M)$ and $K$ is previsible, then $K\in L^2_{bc}(H\cdot M)$ if and only if $HK\in L^2_{bc}(M)$, and then $K\cdot(H\cdot M)=(KH)\cdot M$.

:::

::: {.proof}

1. Localise: with $T_n=\inf\{t:\int_0^tH^2d\langle M\rangle\ge n\}\wedge S_n$ (and $S_n$ as in @prp-localising-by-hitting-times) one has $H\in L^2(M^{T_n})$, so $H\cdot M^{T_n}$ is defined by @thm-ito-isometry.

2. By the stopping property in @thm-ito-isometry the integrals for different $n$ are consistent, which defines $H\cdot M$ on all of $[0,\infty)$; it is a continuous local martingale reduced by $(T_n)$. Parts 2 and 3 and uniqueness are then checked on each localised piece.

:::

::: {#def-locally-bounded .definition name="Locally bounded previsible process"}

A previsible $H$ is *locally bounded* if $\sup_{s\le t}|H_s|<\infty$ almost surely for every $t$. Continuous adapted processes are locally bounded, and then $\int_0^t|H_s||dA_s|<\infty$ for every finite variation $A$.

:::

::: {#def-integral-semimartingale .definition name="Integral against a semi-martingale"}

For a continuous semi-martingale $X=X_0+M+A$ and a locally bounded previsible $H$, put $H\cdot X=H\cdot M+H\cdot A$, the first an Itô integral and the second a Lebesgue–Stieltjes integral. Write $(H\cdot X)_t=\int_0^tH_s\,dX_s$.

:::

::: {#prp-stochastic-integral-properties .proposition name="Properties of the integral"}

For locally bounded previsible $H,K$ and semi-martingales $X$:

1. $(H,X)\mapsto H\cdot X$ is bilinear.

2. $H\cdot(K\cdot X)=(HK)\cdot X$.

3. $(H\cdot X)^T=H\mathbf 1_{[0,T]}\cdot X=H\cdot X^T$ for every stopping time $T$.

4. If $X$ is a continuous local martingale (resp. finite variation), so is $H\cdot X$.

5. If $H=\sum H_{i-1}\mathbf 1_{(t_{i-1},t_i]}$ with $H_{i-1}\in\mathcal F_{t_{i-1}}$ (not necessarily bounded), then $(H\cdot X)_t=\sum H_{i-1}(X_{t_i\wedge t}-X_{t_{i-1}\wedge t})$.

:::

::: {#prp-stochastic-dominated-convergence .proposition name="Stochastic dominated convergence"}

Let $X$ be a continuous semi-martingale and $H^n$ previsible with $H^n\to H$ pointwise and $|H^n|\le K$ for a locally bounded previsible $K$. Then $H^n\cdot X\to H\cdot X$ u.c.p.

:::

::: {#prp-integral-riemann-sums .proposition name="The integral as a limit of Riemann sums"}

If $X$ is a continuous semi-martingale and $H$ is adapted, left-continuous with right limits, then
$$\int_0^tH_s\,dX_s=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{\lceil2^nt\rceil}H_{(i-1)2^{-n}}\big(X_{i2^{-n}\wedge t}-X_{(i-1)2^{-n}\wedge t}\big)\quad\text{u.c.p.}$$

:::

This follows from stochastic dominated convergence applied to the left-endpoint approximations of $H$ (proofs of the last two propositions omitted).
