For complex matrices, the symmetry $A^{\mathsf T}=A$ that produces real eigenvalues changes to $\bar A^{\mathsf T}=A$. From $\det(A-\lambda I)=0$, find the eigenvalues of the 2 by 2 "Hermitian" matrix
$$A=\begin{bmatrix}4&2+i\\2-i&0\end{bmatrix}=\bar A^{\mathsf T}.$$
To see why eigenvalues are real when $\bar A^{\mathsf T}=A$, adjust equation (1) of the text to $\bar A\bar{\mathbf{x}}=\bar\lambda\bar{\mathbf{x}}$.

Transpose to $\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathsf T}\bar A^{\mathsf T}=\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathsf T}\bar\lambda$. With $\bar A^{\mathsf T}=A$, reach equation (2): $\lambda=\bar\lambda$.
