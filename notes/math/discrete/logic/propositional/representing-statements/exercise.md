::: {.problem #prob-propositional-15 origin="external" source="fit1058-cn-4.21-q15"}
A meeting at NASA in 1969 may include Judith Cohen, Margaret Hamilton and
Katherine Johnson. Let *Judith*, *Margaret*, *Katherine*
be the propositions that each is in the meeting. For each statement, write a
proposition in CNF with the same meaning.

(a) Judith and Margaret are not both in the meeting.

(b) Either Judith or Margaret, but not both, is in the meeting.

(c) At least one of the three is in the meeting.

(d) At most one of the three is in the meeting.

(e) Exactly one of the three is in the meeting.

(f) At least two of the three are in the meeting.

(g) At most two of the three are in the meeting.

(h) Exactly two of the three are in the meeting.

(i) Exactly three of the three are in the meeting.

(j) None of the three is in the meeting.
:::

$$\begin{aligned}

        \wedge(\neg\mathrm{Hagrid}\vee\mathrm{Norberta})
        \wedge(\neg\mathrm{Fred}\vee\mathrm{George})\wedge(\mathrm{Fred}\vee\neg\mathrm{George})\\

        \wedge(\neg\mathrm{Voldemort}\vee\neg\mathrm{Dolores})
        \wedge(\neg\mathrm{Bellatrix}\vee\neg\mathrm{Dolores})
    \end{aligned}$$

disjuncts would it have?
