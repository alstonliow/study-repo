::: {.problem #prob-counting-02 origin="external" source="fit1058-cn-8.10-q2"}
(a) A House of Representatives ballot paper has $n$ boxes, one per
    candidate. A voter must enter the numbers $1$ to $n$ in order of
    preference, exactly one number per box. In how many ways can this
    be done?
(b) A Senate ballot paper has one box for each of $N$ candidates, but
    voters need only enter the numbers $1$ to $12$ for their twelve most
    preferred candidates. In how many ways can this be done?
:::

::: {.problem #prob-counting-10 origin="external" source="fit1058-cn-8.10-q10"}
How many strings of five digits are there in which

(a) there is no restriction whatsoever;
(b) leading 0s are not allowed;
(c) all digits are identical;
(d) all digits are different;
(e) every digit is different to its predecessor (other repetitions
    allowed);
(f) all digits are in strictly ascending order;
(g) all digits are in nondecreasing order;
(h) the ordering of digits is monotonic (either nondecreasing or
    nonincreasing);
(i) the digits are in arithmetic progression;
(j) the digits are in geometric progression;
(k) the digits add up to a multiple of 9;
(l) no digit appears in its own position (the first digit is not 1,
    the second is not 2, and so on);
(m) in addition to (l), all the digits are different.
:::
