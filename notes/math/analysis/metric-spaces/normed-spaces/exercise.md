::: {.problem #prob-camib-ats-2-q11 origin="external" source="camib-ats-2-q11"}
On a normed vector space there is a canonical choice of metric which makes it a metric space: $d(x,y) = \|x - y\|$. Show that on a finite-dimensional real vector space, every choice of norm (and thus canonical metric) is equivalent. Is this still true for infinite-dimensional vector spaces?
:::

::: {.problem #prob-camii-lans-1-q7 origin="external" source="camii-lans-1-q7"}
Let $X$ be a normed space. For $x\in X\setminus\{0\}$ write $\pi(x)=x/\|x\|$. Is it true that $\|\pi(x)-\pi(y)\|\le\|x-y\|$ whenever $\|x\|,\ \|y\|\ge1$?
:::

::: {.problem #prob-camii-lans-1-q10 origin="external" source="camii-lans-1-q10"}

:::

:::

::: {.problem #prob-camii-lans-1-q12 origin="external" source="camii-lans-1-q12"}
Assume that the vector space $X$ is the (algebraic) direct sum of subspaces $Y$ and $Z$, i.e., $Y\cap Z=\{0\}$ and $X=Y+Z$. Let $\|\cdot\|$ and $\|\cdot\|'$ be two norms on $X$ that are equivalent on $Y$ and on $Z$. Must they be equivalent on $X$?
:::
