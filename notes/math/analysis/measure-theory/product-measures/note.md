Let $(E_1,\mathcal E_1,\mu_1)$ and $(E_2,\mathcal E_2,\mu_2)$ be σ-finite measure spaces. We build a measure on $E_1\times E_2$ that gives a rectangle the product of its side measures, and show that integrals against it may be computed as iterated integrals in either order.

::: {#def-product-sigma-algebra .definition name="Product σ-algebra"}

The *measurable rectangles* $A_1\times A_2$ ($A_i\in\mathcal E_i$) form a π-system on $E_1\times E_2$. The σ-algebra they generate is the *product σ-algebra*

$$
\mathcal E_1\otimes\mathcal E_2=\sigma\big(\{A_1\times A_2:\ A_1\in\mathcal E_1,\ A_2\in\mathcal E_2\}\big).
$$

:::

It is the σ-algebra generated by the two coordinate projections. For Borel sets, $\mathcal B(\mathbb R)\otimes\mathcal B(\mathbb R)=\mathcal B(\mathbb R^2)$, since open sets of $\mathbb R^2$ are countable unions of open rectangles with rational corners.

::: {#lem-sections-measurable .lemma name="Sections and partial integrals are measurable"}

Let $f:E_1\times E_2\to[0,\infty]$ be $\mathcal E_1\otimes\mathcal E_2$-measurable. Then

1. for each $x_1\in E_1$, the section $x_2\mapsto f(x_1,x_2)$ is $\mathcal E_2$-measurable;
2. $x_1\mapsto\int_{E_2}f(x_1,x_2)\,\mu_2(dx_2)$ is $\mathcal E_1$-measurable.

The same holds with the roles of $1$ and $2$ exchanged.

:::

::: {.proof}

1. The map $x_2\mapsto(x_1,x_2)$ is measurable: the preimage of a rectangle $A_1\times A_2$ is $A_2$ or $\emptyset$. Compose it with $f$.

2. First let $\mu_2$ be finite. Let $V$ be the set of bounded measurable $f$ for which part 2 holds. It is a vector space, contains $\mathbf 1_{A_1\times A_2}$ (the partial integral is $\mu_2(A_2)\mathbf 1_{A_1}$), and is closed under bounded increasing limits by monotone convergence. By the monotone class theorem (@thm-monotone-class) $V$ contains all bounded measurable functions.

3. A measurable $f\ge0$ is the increasing limit of the bounded functions $f\wedge n$; partial integrals converge by monotone convergence, and limits of measurable functions are measurable.

4. For σ-finite $\mu_2$, write $E_2=\bigcup_kF_k$ with $F_k$ disjoint of finite measure. The partial integral is $\sum_k\int_{F_k}f(x_1,x_2)\,\mu_2(dx_2)$, a sum of measurable functions by step 3 applied to the finite measures $\mu_2(\,\cdot\cap F_k)$.

:::

::: {#thm-product-measure .theorem name="Product measure"}

There is a unique measure $\mu=\mu_1\otimes\mu_2$ on $\mathcal E_1\otimes\mathcal E_2$ with

$$
\mu(A_1\times A_2)=\mu_1(A_1)\,\mu_2(A_2)\qquad\text{for all }A_i\in\mathcal E_i .
$$

It is given by $\mu(A)=\int_{E_1}\Big(\int_{E_2}\mathbf 1_A(x_1,x_2)\,\mu_2(dx_2)\Big)\mu_1(dx_1)$, and also by the same formula with the order of integration reversed.

:::

::: {.proof}

1. The formula makes sense by @lem-sections-measurable. $\mu(\emptyset)=0$, and for disjoint $A^{(n)}$ with union $A$, $\mathbf 1_A=\sum_n\mathbf 1_{A^{(n)}}$; integrating term by term twice (@cor-series-nonnegative-integral) gives $\mu(A)=\sum_n\mu(A^{(n)})$. So $\mu$ is a measure.

2. For a rectangle the inner integral is $\mu_2(A_2)\mathbf 1_{A_1}(x_1)$, so $\mu(A_1\times A_2)=\mu_1(A_1)\mu_2(A_2)$. The reversed formula defines a measure with the same property.

3. Choose $E_i=\bigcup_kF^i_k$ with $\mu_i(F^i_k)<\infty$. The rectangles $F^1_j\times F^2_k$ cover $E_1\times E_2$, lie in the π-system of rectangles and have finite measure. By @cor-uniqueness-sigma-finite any two measures with the product property coincide; in particular the two orders of integration give the same $\mu$.

:::

::: {#thm-fubini .theorem name="Fubini–Tonelli theorem"}

1. (Tonelli) For measurable $f:E_1\times E_2\to[0,\infty]$,
$$\int f\,d(\mu_1\otimes\mu_2)=\int_{E_1}\Big(\int_{E_2}f\,d\mu_2\Big)d\mu_1=\int_{E_2}\Big(\int_{E_1}f\,d\mu_1\Big)d\mu_2 .$$

2. (Fubini) If $f$ is $\mu_1\otimes\mu_2$-integrable, then for $\mu_1$-almost every $x_1$ the section $f(x_1,\cdot)$ is $\mu_2$-integrable, the a.e.-defined function $x_1\mapsto\int_{E_2}f(x_1,x_2)\,\mu_2(dx_2)$ is $\mu_1$-integrable, and the same two identities hold.

:::

::: {.proof}

1. For $f=\mathbf 1_A$ part 1 is @thm-product-measure. By linearity it holds for simple $f$, and by monotone convergence, applied three times along simple $f_n\uparrow f$ (inner integrals increase by monotone convergence, then outer ones), for every measurable $f\ge0$.

2. Let $f$ be integrable. By part 1 applied to $|f|$, $\int_{E_1}\big(\int_{E_2}|f|\,d\mu_2\big)d\mu_1<\infty$, so the inner integral of $|f|$ is finite outside a $\mu_1$-null set $N$ (@thm-integral-linear-monotone).

3. For $x_1\notin N$, both $f^+(x_1,\cdot)$ and $f^-(x_1,\cdot)$ are integrable, and $\int f(x_1,\cdot)\,d\mu_2=\int f^+(x_1,\cdot)\,d\mu_2-\int f^-(x_1,\cdot)\,d\mu_2$. Set it to $0$ on $N$.

4. Apply part 1 to $f^+$ and to $f^-$ (each finite since $f$ is integrable) and subtract: the iterated integral equals $\int f^+-\int f^-=\int f$. The other order is symmetric.

:::

::: {#exm-fubini-needs-integrability .example name="Integrability cannot be dropped"}

On $(0,1)^2$ with Lebesgue measure let $f(x,y)=\dfrac{x^2-y^2}{(x^2+y^2)^2}=\dfrac{\partial}{\partial y}\Big(\dfrac{y}{x^2+y^2}\Big)$. For fixed $x$, $\int_0^1f(x,y)\,dy=\dfrac1{1+x^2}$, so $\int_0^1\!\int_0^1f\,dy\,dx=\dfrac\pi4$. By the antisymmetry $f(y,x)=-f(x,y)$ the other order gives $-\dfrac\pi4$. Tonelli shows $\int|f|=\infty$.

:::

The product of $n$ σ-finite measures is built by repeating the construction, and it is associative: $(\mu_1\otimes\mu_2)\otimes\mu_3=\mu_1\otimes(\mu_2\otimes\mu_3)$, both being determined by their values on boxes. Lebesgue measure on $\mathbb R^d$ is $\lambda\otimes\dots\otimes\lambda$.
