::: {.problem #prob-cama1s-3-q7 origin="external" source="cama1s-3-q7"}
Let $f$ be continuous on $[-1,1]$ and twice differentiable on $(-1,1)$. Let
$$\varphi(x) = \begin{cases} \dfrac{f(x) - f(0)}{x}, & x \neq 0 \\ f'(0), & x = 0 \end{cases}$$

(a) Show that $\varphi$ is continuous on $[-1,1]$ and differentiable on $(-1,1)$.

(b) Using a second order mean value theorem for $f$, show that $\varphi'(x) = f''(\theta x)/2$ for some $\theta \in (0,1)$. Hence prove that there exists $c \in (-1,1)$ with $f''(c) = f(-1) + f(1) - 2f(0)$.
:::

::: {.problem #prob-cama1s-3-q8 origin="external" source="cama1s-3-q8"}
Let $f : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ be defined by $f(x) = \exp(-1/x^2)$ for $x \neq 0$ and $f(0) = 0$. Show that $f$ is continuous and differentiable. Show that $f$ is twice differentiable. Indeed, show that $f$ is infinitely differentiable, and that $f^{(n)}(0) = 0$ for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$. Comment, in the light of what you know about Taylor series. [You may assume standard properties of the exponential function.]
:::
